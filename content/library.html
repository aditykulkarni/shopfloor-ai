1. "What do you have for AI?" talk track

The question usually means one of three things: "are you keeping up", "can you solve my problem", or "is this safe". Answer all three in under two minutes.

  1. Reframe to their problem. "Before products, can I ask where engineering or the plant loses the most time today? AI is worth it where there is a lot of repeated judgement on data you already have."
  2. Three layers. "We think about AI in three layers. First, assistants that answer from your PLM data and documents with citations. Second, agents that do engineering tasks such as change impact analysis, with a person approving anything that changes data. Third, open connection through the Model Context Protocol, so whatever AI tools you choose can reach PLM under your access rules."
  3. Proof. "Here is a two-minute demo on sample data: a question with cited documents, then a change impact draft that waits for approval."
  4. Safety. "Every answer is traceable, every write needs approval, access follows PLM roles, and the model can run in your own environment."
  5. Next step. "Pick one use case, one owner and twenty real questions. In six weeks we measure it against today's process and decide together."

Annual value = users × minutes ÷ 60 × days × hourly cost × adoption = 120 × 10 ÷ 60 × 220 × 900 × 0.6 = ₹23.8 lakh. Payback = 40 ÷ (23.8 − 8) ≈ 2.5 years. Always add the break-even sentence: "This pays back within three years if each user saves at least N minutes a day." Use valuecase.py from Unit 22 for sensitivity and the tornado chart.

5. Objection handling

ObjectionResponse
"It will hallucinate.""It can. So it answers only from your documents, cites them, refuses when they do not cover the question, and we measure that on your own questions before go-live."
"Our data cannot leave the plant.""Then the model runs in your environment. Open models are good enough for many of these tasks, and we compare options on your evaluation set."
"Our BOM data is not clean enough.""Most companies say that. A reconciliation report takes days and tells us exactly where it matters. It is often the first win."
"We tried a pilot and it went nowhere.""Usually that is an owner and exit criteria problem, not a model problem. Let us agree both before we start."
"What if the agent changes something wrong?""It cannot change anything without a named approver. Reads are open, writes are gated in code, and everything is logged."
"Why not just use ChatGPT or Copilot?""Use them. With MCP they can reach PLM under your access rules. The value is the connection to engineering data, not the chat window."
"How do we know it is worth it?""We measure today's process first, then the pilot, and decide on the conservative number."

6. MCP control set for the security review

  • Registry. Only approved servers, with an owner, a version and a fingerprint of every tool definition.
  • Identity. Remote servers use OAuth with the user's own identity; no shared service accounts for user requests.
  • Least privilege. Read and write split into separate servers or tools; roles mapped from PLM.
  • Annotations and approval. Hosts require approval for every tool that is not read-only.
  • Validation. Strict parameter schemas and patterns; errors that do not leak internals.
  • Audit. Who called what, with which arguments, what came back, and who approved.
  • Change control. Fingerprint check in CI; a changed definition needs a new review.
  • Testing. Contract tests, security tests by role and prompt injection tests on every release.

7. Agentic AI in one answer

"An agent is a model that can use tools in steps to finish a task: look up the part, check where it is used, read the open changes, check stock, then draft the impact report. The model proposes each step; our code runs it, checks it and logs it. Anything that changes data waits for a named person. We raise autonomy only when the measurements say it is safe."

8. Generative AI in one answer

"Generative AI produces new content, such as text, code or images, by predicting what comes next from patterns learned in huge amounts of data. On its own it does not know your products. Connected to your PLM documents and data, and measured on your questions, it becomes a fast, cited assistant for engineers. Generative design is different: it uses physics-based optimisation to propose shapes, and it is mature and proven today."
